3. Item Construction Standard Applied
• Every item contains exactly five options (A–E) and one unambiguously best answer.
• Difficulty reflects reasoning depth, not confusing wording. Easy = foundation/one-step; Moderate = typical IMAT application; Hard = upper-end IMAT or controlled Ahsora Challenge.
• The item pool is aligned to Ahsora’s FSc foundation + A-Level conceptual extension + IMAT application model; Ahsora Challenge items are only slightly above normal IMAT depth.
• Each item is tagged by chapter, topic, difficulty and question type.
• The Hard pool contains 7 Ahsora Challenge items (28% of 25 Hard questions); the remaining 18 are upper-end IMAT.

• Every item includes a concise mechanism/reasoning explanation and must still undergo independent scientific QA before publication.
4. Animal Tissues & Homeostasis
12 MCQs  •  6 Easy  •  3 Moderate  •  3 Hard
Major animal tissue types
BIO-HAP-TIS-001  |  Easy  |  Recall / Knowledge
Which tissue type forms continuous sheets that cover body surfaces and line many internal cavities?
A. Epithelial tissue
B. Connective tissue
C. Nervous tissue
D. Skeletal muscle tissue
E. Blood
Correct: A — Epithelial tissue
Explanation: Epithelia form continuous protective, absorptive or secretory surfaces and linings.
BIO-HAP-TIS-002  |  Easy  |  Conceptual
A tissue contains relatively few cells embedded in abundant extracellular matrix. Which major tissue class best fits this description?
A. Epithelial tissue
B. Connective tissue
C. Nervous tissue
D. Cardiac muscle tissue
E. Smooth muscle tissue
Correct: B — Connective tissue
Explanation: Connective tissues characteristically contain cells dispersed within extracellular matrix.
BIO-HAP-TIS-003  |  Easy  |  Recall / Knowledge
Which major tissue type is specialised for rapid electrical communication over long distances in the body?
A. Epithelial tissue
B. Adipose tissue
C. Nervous tissue
D. Dense connective tissue
E. Cartilage
Correct: C — Nervous tissue
Explanation: Neurons and supporting nervous tissue are specialised for electrical signalling and information transfer.
BIO-HAP-TIS-008  |  Moderate  |  Application
A biopsy shows tightly packed cells resting on a basement membrane, with little extracellular matrix and rapid renewal after abrasion. Which tissue is most likely being examined?
A. Tendon
B. Bone
C. Epithelium
D. Adipose tissue
E. Nervous tissue
Correct: C — Epithelium
Explanation: Tightly packed renewing cells with little matrix and a basement membrane are characteristic of epithelium.
Homeostasis and negative feedback
BIO-HAP-TIS-004  |  Easy  |  Recall / Knowledge
In a negative-feedback system, what is the usual effect of the response on the original disturbance?
A. It amplifies the disturbance until a new state is reached.
B. It permanently eliminates all variation in the variable.
C. It changes the receptor so the stimulus is no longer detected.
D. It opposes the disturbance and moves the variable toward its set range.
E. It always raises the regulated variable above normal.
Correct: D — It opposes the disturbance and moves the variable toward its set range.
Explanation: Negative feedback counters a deviation, helping return a variable toward its physiological range.
BIO-HAP-TIS-005  |  Easy  |  Conceptual
In a homeostatic control loop, which component detects a change in the regulated variable?
A. An effector
B. A target organ only
C. A structural protein
D. An antibody
E. A receptor or sensor
Correct: E — A receptor or sensor
Explanation: Receptors detect changes in regulated variables and provide input to the control system.
BIO-HAP-TIS-007  |  Moderate  |  Application
A person enters a cold environment. Skin thermoreceptors detect the fall in temperature, and skeletal muscles begin to shiver. In this control loop, shivering is best described as
A. the receptor that detects the temperature change.
B. an effector response that increases heat production.
C. the control centre that establishes the set point.
D. a positive-feedback response that lowers body temperature further.
E. the initial stimulus that causes heat loss.
Correct: B — an effector response that increases heat production.
Explanation: Shivering is an effector response that raises metabolic heat production after cold is detected.
Positive versus negative feedback
BIO-HAP-TIS-006  |  Easy  |  Conceptual
Which process is a classic example of positive rather than negative feedback?
A. Increasing uterine contractions triggered by oxytocin during labour
B. Regulation of blood glucose after a meal
C. Control of core body temperature during exercise
D. Adjustment of ventilation to carbon dioxide levels
E. Maintenance of water balance by ADH
Correct: A — Increasing uterine contractions triggered by oxytocin during labour
Explanation: Oxytocin-driven labour contractions amplify the initiating stimulus, making them a positive-feedback example.
Integration of organ systems
BIO-HAP-TIS-009  |  Moderate  |  Integrated
During vigorous exercise, body temperature and carbon dioxide production both rise. Which response best illustrates coordinated homeostasis?
A. Reduced skin blood flow and reduced ventilation conserve heat and carbon dioxide.
B. Increased insulin secretion is the primary mechanism for removing carbon dioxide.
C. Decreased heart rate is required to restore both variables.
D. Increased skin blood flow and ventilation help remove heat and carbon dioxide.
E. Positive feedback deliberately raises temperature until exercise ends.
Correct: D — Increased skin blood flow and ventilation help remove heat and carbon dioxide.
Explanation: Exercise requires simultaneous regulation of heat and CO2, so ventilation and skin blood flow rise together.
BIO-HAP-TIS-012  |  Hard  |  Integrated  |  AHSORA CHALLENGE
A drug blocks autonomic vasodilation in skin vessels but does not affect sweating. During exercise in a hot environment, which change most directly reduces the person’s ability to lose heat?
A. Sweat glands can no longer secrete water.
B. Less warm blood reaches the skin surface for heat transfer.
C. Core temperature cannot be detected by receptors.
D. Skeletal muscles can no longer generate ATP.
E. Blood glucose can no longer be regulated.
Correct: B — Less warm blood reaches the skin surface for heat transfer.
Explanation: Skin vasodilation normally brings warm blood near the surface; blocking it reduces convective and radiative heat loss.
Homeostatic control loops
BIO-HAP-TIS-010  |  Hard  |  Integrated  |  Upper-end IMAT
A homeostatic variable changes normally, receptors detect the change, and the control centre sends an appropriate signal, but the regulated variable fails to return toward normal. Which defect is most directly suggested?
A. Failure of the receptor to detect the change
B. Failure of the control centre to receive sensory information
C. Absence of any initial disturbance
D. Excessive positive feedback at the receptor
E. Failure of the effector to produce the required response
Correct: E — Failure of the effector to produce the required response
Explanation: If sensing and control are intact but correction fails, the downstream effector is the most likely failed component.
BIO-HAP-TIS-011  |  Hard  |  Application  |  Upper-end IMAT
A mutation causes a temperature sensor to report 37.0°C when the actual core temperature is 39.0°C. Other components of thermoregulation are intact. What is the most likely consequence?
A. Heat-loss responses will be inappropriately reduced because the control system underestimates the disturbance.
B. Heat-loss responses will be maximal because the control system detects severe hyperthermia.
C. Shivering will necessarily stop all heat production.
D. The set point will automatically fall to 35.0°C.
E. Positive feedback will always restore the correct sensor reading.
Correct: A — Heat-loss responses will be inappropriately reduced because the control system underestimates the disturbance.
Explanation: An under-reading temperature sensor reduces the error signal, so heat-loss responses are insufficient.

Which enzyme begins the digestion of starch in the mouth?
A. Pepsin
B. Lipase
C. Trypsin

Correct: D — Salivary amylase
Explanation: Salivary amylase begins hydrolysis of starch before food reaches the stomach.
Mechanical versus chemical digestion
BIO-HAP-DIG-003  |  Easy  |  Conceptual
What is the main digestive role of bile salts?
A. They hydrolyse proteins into amino acids.
B. They convert starch directly into glucose.
C. They neutralise all acid in the bloodstream.
D. They digest nucleic acids in the stomach.
E. They emulsify fat, increasing the surface area available to lipase.
Correct: E — They emulsify fat, increasing the surface area available to lipase.
Explanation: Bile salts emulsify lipids into smaller droplets, increasing surface area for lipase action.
Absorption and villus adaptations
BIO-HAP-DIG-004  |  Easy  |  Conceptual
Which feature of small-intestinal villi most directly increases the rate of nutrient absorption?

D. Absence of membrane transport proteins
E. Storage of digestive enzymes inside red blood cells
Correct: A — A very large surface area created by villi and microvilli
Explanation: Villi and microvilli greatly increase absorptive membrane area.
Digestive enzymes and pH
BIO-HAP-DIG-005  |  Moderate  |  Application
Pepsin functions well in the acidic stomach but becomes much less active in the alkaline small intestine. The best explanation is that a change in pH alters
A. the number of peptide bonds in the substrate before digestion.
B. the ionisation and shape of the enzyme’s active site.
C. the genetic code of the enzyme molecules.
D. the amount of bile stored in the gallbladder.
E. the number of villi lining the small intestine.
Correct: B — the ionisation and shape of the enzyme’s active site.
Explanation: Enzyme activity depends on active-site ionisation and shape, both of which can be altered by pH.
Liver, pancreas and small intestine
BIO-HAP-DIG-006  |  Moderate  |  Application
A blockage prevents pancreatic bicarbonate from entering the duodenum. Which immediate effect is most likely?
A. Protein digestion in the mouth increases sharply.
B. Bile can no longer be produced by the liver.
C. The duodenal contents remain too acidic for optimal activity of many pancreatic enzymes.
D. Villi lose their blood supply.
E. Starch can no longer be hydrolysed by salivary amylase before swallowing.
Correct: C — The duodenal contents remain too acidic for optimal activity of many pancreatic enzymes.
Explanation: Pancreatic bicarbonate neutralises gastric acid so pancreatic enzymes can work near their optimal pH.
Absorption and membrane transport
BIO-HAP-DIG-007  |  Hard  |  Integrated  |  Upper-end IMAT
A disease selectively destroys microvilli on intestinal epithelial cells while leaving the villi and digestive enzymes intact. Which effect is most likely?
A. Complete failure of chemical digestion in the intestinal lumen
B. Increased absorption because nutrients cross a shorter route through the gut wall
C. Loss of bile production
D. Reduced nutrient absorption because apical membrane surface area is greatly decreased.
E. Loss of gastric acid secretion
Correct: D — Reduced nutrient absorption because apical membrane surface area is greatly decreased.
Explanation: Destroying microvilli sharply reduces apical membrane area, limiting nutrient uptake despite normal luminal digestion.
Fat digestion and absorption
BIO-HAP-DIG-008  |  Hard  |  Integrated  |  Upper-end IMAT
A patient produces normal pancreatic lipase but has almost no bile salts entering the intestine. Which combination is most likely after a fatty meal?
A. Increased starch digestion and increased amino-acid absorption
B. Complete loss of protein digestion but normal lipid digestion
C. Faster lipid digestion because large fat droplets expose more surface area
D. No effect because bile salts are digestive enzymes that can be replaced by lipase
E. Reduced fat emulsification and reduced efficiency of lipid digestion and absorption
Correct: E — Reduced fat emulsification and reduced efficiency of lipid digestion and absorption
Explanation: Without bile salts, fat remains in larger droplets, reducing lipase access and subsequent lipid absorption.
6. Respiratory System & Gas Exchange
12 MCQs  •  6 Easy  •  3 Moderate  •  3 Hard
Airways and alveoli
BIO-HAP-RESP-001  |  Easy  |  Recall / Knowledge
Which structure is the principal site of gas exchange between air and blood in the lungs?
A. Alveoli
B. Trachea
C. Larynx
D. Bronchi
E. Diaphragm
Correct: A — Alveoli
Explanation: Alveoli provide the thin, highly vascular surface where respiratory gases diffuse between air and blood.
Alveolar adaptations
BIO-HAP-RESP-002  |  Easy  |  Conceptual
Why are alveolar walls only one cell thick?
A. A thin wall prevents any water from entering the alveoli.
B. A thin barrier shortens the diffusion distance for oxygen and carbon dioxide.
C. A thin wall allows alveoli to contract like skeletal muscle.
D. A thin wall increases the amount of cartilage supporting each alveolus.
E. A thin wall stops diffusion when blood flow is low.
Correct: B — A thin barrier shortens the diffusion distance for oxygen and carbon dioxide.
Explanation: A one-cell-thick barrier minimises diffusion distance and therefore increases gas-transfer rate.
BIO-HAP-RESP-006  |  Easy  |  Recall / Knowledge
Which feature helps maintain a steep concentration gradient for gases across the alveolar surface?
A. Continuous blood flow through pulmonary capillaries
B. A thick epithelial barrier
C. Very low capillary density
D. Closure of bronchioles during inspiration
E. Absence of haemoglobin
Correct: A — Continuous blood flow through pulmonary capillaries
Explanation: Continuous perfusion removes oxygen and brings CO2, helping preserve steep diffusion gradients.
Ventilation mechanics
BIO-HAP-RESP-003  |  Easy  |  Recall / Knowledge
During quiet inspiration, contraction of the diaphragm causes it to
A. dome upward, decreasing thoracic volume.
B. close the trachea.
C. flatten, increasing thoracic volume.
D. compress the lungs directly from above.
E. force air out by raising intrapulmonary pressure.
Correct: C — flatten, increasing thoracic volume.
Explanation: Diaphragm contraction flattens the muscle, increasing thoracic volume and lowering intrapulmonary pressure.
Gas exchange versus respiration
BIO-HAP-RESP-004  |  Easy  |  Conceptual
Which statement correctly distinguishes ventilation from cellular respiration?
A. Ventilation produces ATP, whereas cellular respiration moves air.
B. Ventilation occurs only in mitochondria, whereas cellular respiration occurs only in alveoli.
C. Both terms mean diffusion of oxygen across an alveolus.
D. Ventilation moves air into and out of the lungs, whereas cellular respiration releases usable energy in cells.
E. Cellular respiration is simply another term for breathing frequency.
Correct: D — Ventilation moves air into and out of the lungs, whereas cellular respiration releases usable energy in cells.
Explanation: Ventilation is bulk airflow, while cellular respiration is intracellular energy-releasing metabolism.
Diffusion gradients
BIO-HAP-RESP-005  |  Easy  |  Conceptual
Oxygen normally diffuses from an alveolus into pulmonary capillary blood because
A. oxygen is actively pumped across the alveolar membrane.
B. carbon dioxide directly pulls oxygen into plasma.
C. red blood cells contain no oxygen-binding pigment.
D. alveolar pressure is always lower than atmospheric pressure.
E. the partial pressure of oxygen is higher in alveolar air than in deoxygenated blood.
Correct: E — the partial pressure of oxygen is higher in alveolar air than in deoxygenated blood.
Explanation: Oxygen diffuses down its partial-pressure gradient from alveolar air into deoxygenated blood.
Exercise and gas exchange
BIO-HAP-RESP-007  |  Moderate  |  Application
During exercise, pulmonary ventilation and cardiac output both increase. How does this support gas exchange?
A. The alveolar membrane becomes much thicker.
B. Fresh alveolar air and deoxygenated blood are supplied more rapidly, helping maintain diffusion gradients.
C. Oxygen begins to move by active transport.
D. Carbon dioxide production in muscles immediately stops.
E. The lungs switch from diffusion to osmosis.
Correct: B — Fresh alveolar air and deoxygenated blood are supplied more rapidly, helping maintain diffusion gradients.
Explanation: Greater ventilation and perfusion continually refresh both sides of the respiratory surface during exercise.
Emphysema and surface area
BIO-HAP-RESP-008  |  Moderate  |  Application
In emphysema, destruction of walls between adjacent alveoli creates larger air spaces. Which change most directly impairs gas exchange?
A. Increase in oxygen solubility in plasma
B. Increase in the number of pulmonary capillaries
C. Loss of total alveolar surface area
D. Reduction in airway resistance to zero
E. Conversion of diffusion into facilitated diffusion
Correct: C — Loss of total alveolar surface area
Explanation: Emphysema merges alveoli and reduces total exchange surface area, lowering diffusion capacity.
Ventilation-perfusion relationships
BIO-HAP-RESP-009  |  Moderate  |  Integrated
A small region of lung is ventilated normally but receives almost no blood flow. What is the main limitation in that region?
A. Carbon dioxide cannot diffuse because alveoli lack air.
B. The diaphragm cannot contract.
C. The region becomes the main site of oxygen uptake for the whole lung.
D. Oxygen entering the alveoli cannot be efficiently transferred to circulating blood.
E. Cellular respiration stops in the airway epithelium.
Correct: D — Oxygen entering the alveoli cannot be efficiently transferred to circulating blood.
Explanation: Ventilation without perfusion cannot transfer much oxygen into the circulating blood.
Diffusion across the respiratory membrane
BIO-HAP-RESP-010  |  Hard  |  Integrated  |  Upper-end IMAT
Two patients have the same alveolar oxygen concentration and pulmonary blood flow. Patient X has twice the alveolar surface area but also twice the diffusion distance of patient Y. Assuming other factors are equal, the opposing changes would tend to
A. both increase oxygen diffusion.
B. both decrease oxygen diffusion.
C. have no relation to diffusion rate.
D. make oxygen require active transport.
E. partly offset each other because diffusion increases with area but decreases with diffusion distance.
Correct: E — partly offset each other because diffusion increases with area but decreases with diffusion distance.
Explanation: Fick-type diffusion increases with surface area but decreases as barrier thickness increases, so the two changes oppose each other.
High-altitude physiology
BIO-HAP-RESP-011  |  Hard  |  Application  |  Upper-end IMAT
A person rapidly ascends to high altitude, where inspired oxygen partial pressure is lower. Before long-term acclimatisation occurs, which change most directly helps preserve oxygen uptake?
A. An increase in ventilation
B. A decrease in ventilation
C. A sudden increase in alveolar membrane thickness
D. A fall in cardiac output to near zero
E. Conversion of haemoglobin into an enzyme
Correct: A — An increase in ventilation
Explanation: Hyperventilation raises alveolar oxygen availability when inspired oxygen partial pressure falls at altitude.
Integrated respiratory physiology
BIO-HAP-RESP-012  |  Hard  |  Data Interpretation  |  AHSORA CHALLENGE
A patient has normal ventilation but severe thickening of the alveolar-capillary membrane. At rest arterial oxygen is mildly reduced; during exercise it falls further. Which explanation is best?
A. Exercise eliminates the oxygen gradient between alveoli and blood.
B. Exercise shortens capillary transit time, so the increased diffusion barrier becomes more limiting.
C. Thicker membranes increase oxygen solubility only during exercise.
D. Red blood cells stop containing haemoglobin at high heart rates.
E. Carbon dioxide can no longer be produced by working muscle.
Correct: B — Exercise shortens capillary transit time, so the increased diffusion barrier becomes more limiting.
Explanation: With a thickened barrier, faster exercise blood flow reduces equilibration time and exposes diffusion limitation.

BIO-HAP-CIRC-007  |  Easy  |  Recall / Knowledge

A. Erythrocytes

C. Bile salts

E. Nephrons

Pulmonary circulation
BIO-HAP-CIRC-008  |  Easy  |  Conceptual
Which vessel carries deoxygenated blood from the heart toward the lungs?
A. Pulmonary vein
B. Aorta
C. Coronary vein only
D. Carotid artery
E. Pulmonary artery

Explanation: The pulmonary artery is unusual because it carries deoxygenated blood away from the right ventricle.
Cardiac cycle
BIO-HAP-CIRC-009  |  Moderate  |  Application

Explanation: When ventricular pressure exceeds atrial pressure, atrioventricular valves close to prevent regurgitation.
Blood vessels and pressure

Why does blood pressure fall substantially as blood passes through arterioles and capillary networks?

Tissue fluid formation
BIO-HAP-CIRC-011  |  Moderate  |  Application
At the arterial end of many capillary beds, tissue fluid forms mainly because

Haemoglobin and oxygen transport

Why is haemoglobin useful for oxygen transport rather than relying on oxygen dissolved only in plasma?

Valve dysfunction
BIO-HAP-CIRC-013  |  Hard  |  Integrated  |  Upper-end IMAT
A damaged left atrioventricular (mitral) valve allows substantial regurgitation during ventricular systole. Which immediate consequence is most likely?
A. All blood is redirected into the pulmonary artery.

A. Oxygen transport by haemoglobin
B. Glomerular filtration
C. Contraction of skeletal muscle
D. Efficient activation and coordination of B-cell antibody responses to many antigens
E. Production of bile salts
Correct: D — Efficient activation and coordination of B-cell antibody responses to many antigens
Explanation: Helper T cells provide key activating signals for many B-cell responses, so their loss weakens humoral immunity.

BIO-HAP-IMM-008  |  Hard  |  Integrated  |  AHSORA CHALLENGE
Two individuals encounter the same pathogen. Person X was vaccinated months earlier; person Y has never encountered the antigen. X clears the infection before major symptoms develop. Which mechanism best explains the difference?
A. X has permanently elevated body temperature that kills all pathogens.
B. Y lacks innate immunity because vaccination creates it.
C. Vaccination causes antibodies to reproduce independently of cells forever.
D. X no longer requires antigen recognition by lymphocytes.
E. Pre-existing memory cells in X expand quickly and generate a rapid specific response.
Correct: E — Pre-existing memory cells in X expand quickly and generate a rapid specific response.
Explanation: Vaccination creates memory cells that expand rapidly on later exposure and can control infection before major symptoms.
