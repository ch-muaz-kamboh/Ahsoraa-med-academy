3. Item Construction Standard Applied
• Every item contains exactly five options (A–E) and one unambiguously best answer.
• Difficulty reflects reasoning depth, not confusing wording. Easy = foundation/one-step; Moderate = typical IMAT application; Hard = upper-end IMAT or controlled Ahsora Challenge.
• The item pool follows the Ahsora FSc foundation + A-Level conceptual extension + IMAT application model.
• All 48 items are original Ahsora production-draft questions; historical IMAT/past-paper wording is not reused as original content.
• The Hard pool contains 4 Ahsora Challenge items (33% of 12 Hard questions) and 8 upper-end normal IMAT items.
• Correct-answer positions are balanced across A–E: A=10, B=10, C=10, D=9, E=9.
• Every question includes a concise explanation and must still undergo independent teacher/scientific QA before publication.

BIO-EVB-001  |  EASY
Chapter: Mutation, Variation & Selection   •   Topic: Gene and chromosome mutations   •   Type: Recall / Knowledge
Which statement best defines a gene mutation?
A. A change in the number of ribosomes in a cell
B. A temporary change in enzyme activity caused by temperature
C. A change in the amount of ATP produced during respiration
D. A change in the nucleotide sequence of DNA within a gene
E. A change in the shape of a protein without any change in DNA
Correct answer: D — A change in the nucleotide sequence of DNA within a gene
Explanation: A gene mutation is a permanent alteration in the DNA nucleotide sequence of a gene.
BIO-EVB-002  |  EASY
Chapter: Mutation, Variation & Selection   •   Topic: Gene and chromosome mutations   •   Type: Recall / Knowledge
Which change is a chromosome-structure mutation rather than a point mutation?
A. Replacement of one nucleotide by another
B. Insertion of one nucleotide into a coding sequence
C. Deletion of a large segment of a chromosome
D. Loss of one hydrogen bond between DNA bases
E. Failure of one tRNA to bind an amino acid
Correct answer: C — Deletion of a large segment of a chromosome
Explanation: Large deletions alter chromosome structure, whereas point mutations affect one or a few nucleotides.
BIO-EVB-003  |  EASY

Which process during meiosis directly creates new combinations of alleles on homologous chromosomes?
A. DNA replication before meiosis
B. Crossing over between non-sister chromatids
C. Cytokinesis after meiosis II
D. Translation of mRNA
E. Binary fission
Correct answer: B — Crossing over between non-sister chromatids
Explanation: Crossing over exchanges DNA between homologous chromosomes and creates new allele combinations.
BIO-EVB-004  |  EASY

Why are mutations important for evolution?
A. They always make an organism better adapted.
B. They prevent natural selection from acting.
C. They can create new alleles that add heritable variation to a population.
D. They ensure every offspring has a unique chromosome number.

Correct answer: C — They can create new alleles that add heritable variation to a population.
Explanation: Mutation is the ultimate source of new alleles on which evolutionary forces can act.
BIO-EVB-005  |  EASY
Chapter: Mutation, Variation & Selection   •   Topic: Natural selection   •   Type: Recall / Knowledge
For natural selection to cause evolutionary change, variation in a trait must be
A. caused only by exercise.

C. restricted to somatic cells and never inherited.
D. unrelated to survival or reproduction.

Correct answer: E — heritable and associated with differences in reproductive success.
Explanation: Natural selection changes populations only when heritable variants differ in reproductive success.

BIO-EVB-012  |  MODERATE

A bacterial population contains rare cells with an antibiotic-resistance allele before treatment. After repeated antibiotic exposure, the allele becomes common. Which explanation is best?

Two heritable phenotypes occur in a population. Over one generation, 200 individuals of phenotype X produce 500 surviving offspring, while 200 individuals of phenotype Y produce 300 surviving offspring. Assuming the difference is repeatable, which phenotype has higher relative fitness?

A bird allele improves survival during winter but reduces mating success during spring. In a stable population the allele remains at an intermediate frequency for many generations. Which explanation is most reasonable?

A PCR reaction contains template DNA, polymerase, nucleotides and buffer, but one of the two primers is omitted. What is the most likely result?
A. Exponential amplification of the intended double-stranded target will fail.
B. The target will still double normally every cycle.
C. Gel electrophoresis will automatically supply the missing primer.
D. Restriction enzymes in the buffer will replace the primer.
E. The target will be translated into protein instead.
Correct answer: A — Exponential amplification of the intended double-stranded target will fail.
Explanation: Two primers flanking opposite strands are required for efficient exponential amplification of a defined double-stranded target.

A point mutation abolishes a restriction-enzyme recognition site in one allele. After digestion, what change is most likely on a gel compared with the normal allele?
A. Every DNA fragment becomes smaller.
B. No DNA can enter the gel.
C. One larger fragment can replace two smaller fragments that would normally be produced by cleavage at that site.
D. The mutant allele must acquire an extra chromosome.
E. All fragments migrate toward the negative electrode.
Correct answer: C — One larger fragment can replace two smaller fragments that would normally be produced by cleavage at that site.
Explanation: Loss of a cleavage site prevents one cut, leaving adjacent DNA segments joined as a larger fragment.

A plasmid has one restriction site inside an antibiotic-resistance gene. Inserting foreign DNA at that site disrupts the gene. How could this help identify recombinant plasmids?
A. Recombinants will necessarily contain no plasmid DNA.
B. Only non-recombinant plasmids can replicate in bacteria.
C. The inserted DNA will convert the antibiotic into a nutrient.
D. Recombinants may lose resistance conferred by that gene, allowing insertional inactivation to distinguish them.
E. Disrupting the resistance gene causes all bacterial chromosomes to be deleted.
Correct answer: D — Recombinants may lose resistance conferred by that gene, allowing insertional inactivation to distinguish them.
Explanation: Insertional inactivation links successful insertion to loss of a selectable phenotype, helping distinguish recombinant from empty vectors.

A negative-control PCR tube containing all reagents except template DNA produces a strong band of the expected size. What is the best interpretation?
A. The reaction was contaminated with target DNA or previously amplified product.
B. The primers have converted themselves into the target gene.
C. A negative control is expected to produce the strongest band.
D. DNA polymerase synthesised the target sequence without any template or primers.
E. The gel must contain only protein rather than DNA.
Correct answer: A — The reaction was contaminated with target DNA or previously amplified product.
Explanation: A target-sized band in a no-template control indicates contamination and makes positive sample results unreliable.

A locus has two alleles that yield PCR products of 300 bp and 500 bp. A diploid individual shows both a 300-bp band and a 500-bp band on the gel. Which genotype is most consistent with the result?
A. The individual is homozygous for the 300-bp allele.
B. The individual is homozygous for the 500-bp allele.
C. The individual lacks the locus entirely.
D. The result proves both alleles are on the same chromosome copy.
E. The individual is heterozygous for the two alleles.
Correct answer: E — The individual is heterozygous for the two alleles.
Explanation: Two allele-specific product sizes in a diploid sample indicate that both alleles are present, consistent with heterozygosity.
