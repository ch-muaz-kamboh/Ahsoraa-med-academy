2. Water, Weak Interactions & Chemical Basis of Life
Quota: 32 MCQs — 16 Easy, 8 Moderate, 8 Hard.
Easy Questions
BIO-BCH-WAT-001 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Water polarity
Why is a water molecule polar?
A. Oxygen attracts the shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen does.
B. The molecule contains ionic bonds between oxygen and hydrogen.
C. Hydrogen atoms carry complete positive charges.
D. The oxygen atom has no lone pairs of electrons.
E. Water molecules are always dissociated into H+ and OH-.
Correct answer: A — Oxygen attracts the shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen does. Explanation: The O-H bonds are polar because oxygen is more electronegative, giving water a partial charge separation.
BIO-BCH-WAT-002 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Hydrogen bonding
A hydrogen bond between two water molecules forms mainly between:
A. two oxygen atoms sharing a pair of electrons.
B. two hydrogen atoms with opposite full charges.
C. a hydrogen atom and a covalently bonded carbon atom.
D. two hydroxide ions in solution.
E. a partially positive hydrogen of one molecule and a partially negative oxygen of another.
Correct answer: E — a partially positive hydrogen of one molecule and a partially negative oxygen of another. Explanation: Hydrogen bonds arise from attraction between partial charges on neighboring polar molecules.
BIO-BCH-WAT-003 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Hydrophilic substances
Which substance would generally be most hydrophilic?
A. A long hydrocarbon chain
B. A molecule containing several exposed hydroxyl groups
C. A neutral lipid with no polar head group
D. A molecule composed almost entirely of C-H bonds
E. A wax molecule
Correct answer: B — A molecule containing several exposed hydroxyl groups Explanation: Polar hydroxyl groups can hydrogen-bond with water, increasing hydrophilicity.
BIO-BCH-WAT-004 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Hydrophobic effect
Hydrophobic molecules tend to cluster together in water mainly because:
A. they form strong ionic bonds with one another.
B. water molecules become covalently attached to them.
C. they are strongly attracted to water molecules.
D. their clustering reduces disruption of the hydrogen-bonded water network.
E. their nonpolar groups become electrically charged.
Correct answer: D — their clustering reduces disruption of the hydrogen-bonded water network. Explanation: Nonpolar groups aggregate because this minimizes their exposure to water and preserves water-water interactions.
BIO-BCH-WAT-005 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Specific heat
Which property of water helps organisms resist rapid changes in body temperature?
A. High specific heat capacity
B. Low boiling point
C. Low heat of vaporization
D. Complete nonpolarity
E. Inability to form hydrogen bonds
Correct answer: A — High specific heat capacity Explanation: Water absorbs substantial heat before its temperature changes greatly because many hydrogen bonds must be disrupted.
BIO-BCH-WAT-006 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Heat of vaporization
Evaporation of sweat cools the body because water has:
A. a very low specific heat.
B. a high heat of vaporization.
C. no intermolecular attractions.
D. a low surface tension.
E. a low latent heat.
Correct answer: B — a high heat of vaporization. Explanation: Removing the highest-energy water molecules during evaporation carries substantial heat away from the skin.
BIO-BCH-WAT-007 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Solvent properties
Why does sodium chloride dissolve readily in water?
A. Water converts sodium and chloride atoms into isotopes.
B. NaCl becomes covalently bonded to water.
C. Polar water molecules surround and stabilize the separated ions.
D. Water is nonpolar like the crystal lattice.
E. Hydrogen bonds form directly between Na+ and Cl-.
Correct answer: C — Polar water molecules surround and stabilize the separated ions. Explanation: Water's partial charges form hydration shells around Na+ and Cl-, weakening ionic attractions in the crystal.
BIO-BCH-WAT-008 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Cohesion
The tendency of water molecules to stick to one another is called:
A. adhesion.
B. hydrolysis.
C. cohesion.
D. ionization.
E. denaturation.
Correct answer: C — cohesion. Explanation: Cohesion results largely from hydrogen bonding among water molecules.
BIO-BCH-WAT-009 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Ice density
Why does ice float on liquid water?
A. Hydrogen bonds hold water molecules in a more open lattice in ice.
B. Ice contains fewer oxygen atoms than liquid water.
C. Covalent O-H bonds break when water freezes.
D. Frozen water loses all hydrogen bonds.
E. Ice molecules have lower molecular mass.
Correct answer: A — Hydrogen bonds hold water molecules in a more open lattice in ice. Explanation: The open hydrogen-bonded lattice of ice occupies more volume and therefore has lower density than liquid water.
BIO-BCH-WAT-010 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Weak interactions
Which interaction is individually weak but collectively important in stabilizing biological macromolecules?
A. Hydrogen bonding
B. Peptide bonding
C. Phosphodiester bonding
D. Disulfide bonding
E. Glycosidic bonding
Correct answer: A — Hydrogen bonding Explanation: Many weak noncovalent interactions can collectively stabilize proteins, DNA and membranes.
BIO-BCH-WAT-011 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Water in hydrolysis
In a hydrolysis reaction, water is used to:
A. form a peptide bond without releasing any molecule.
B. remove all oxygen atoms from a polymer.
C. break a covalent bond by contributing H and OH to the products.
D. convert a polar molecule into a lipid.
E. increase the number of monomers by dehydration.
Correct answer: C — break a covalent bond by contributing H and OH to the products. Explanation: Hydrolysis cleaves a bond by adding the components of water across it.
BIO-BCH-WAT-012 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Water in condensation
A condensation reaction that joins two monomers usually:
A. requires the polymer to gain one oxygen atom from air.
B. consumes a molecule of carbon dioxide.
C. releases a molecule of water.
D. breaks a polymer into smaller units.
E. produces hydroxide ions as the main product.
Correct answer: C — releases a molecule of water. Explanation: Condensation joins monomers while removing H and OH as water.
BIO-BCH-WAT-013 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: pH concept
A decrease in pH indicates:
A. a decrease in hydrogen ion concentration.
B. an increase in hydrogen ion concentration.
C. a complete absence of hydroxide ions.
D. an increase in temperature only.
E. the solution has become nonpolar.
Correct answer: B — an increase in hydrogen ion concentration. Explanation: pH falls as [H+] rises because pH is inversely related logarithmically to hydrogen ion concentration.
BIO-BCH-WAT-014 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: Protein weak interactions
Which interaction can form between oppositely charged amino-acid side chains in a protein?
A. A phosphodiester bond
B. A glycosidic bond
C. An ionic interaction (salt bridge)
D. A peptide bond between side chains
E. A covalent bond between every charged group
Correct answer: C — An ionic interaction (salt bridge) Explanation: Oppositely charged side chains can attract electrostatically to form a salt bridge.
BIO-BCH-WAT-015 | Easy | Recall / Conceptual | Topic: DNA hydrogen bonds
In double-stranded DNA, hydrogen bonds form directly between:
A. adjacent phosphate groups in one strand.
B. ribose sugars in opposite strands.
C. amino acids and nucleotides.
D. fatty acids in the backbone.
E. complementary nitrogenous bases.
Correct answer: E — complementary nitrogenous bases. Explanation: Complementary bases pair through hydrogen bonds, whereas the sugar-phosphate backbone is covalent.
BIO-BCH-WAT-016 | Easy | Conceptual | Topic: Membrane hydrophobic interactions
In a phospholipid bilayer, hydrophobic fatty-acid tails are oriented mainly:
A. toward the extracellular fluid only.
B. toward the interior of the bilayer, away from water.
C. toward the cytosol only.
D. randomly with no preferred orientation.
E. toward water because hydrocarbon chains are polar.
Correct answer: B — toward the interior of the bilayer, away from water. Explanation: The hydrophobic effect drives nonpolar tails away from water while polar heads face aqueous environments.
Moderate Questions
BIO-BCH-WAT-017 | Moderate | Application | Topic: Hydrophobic effect
A globular protein contains a hydrophobic amino acid that is normally buried in its core. If it is replaced by a strongly charged residue, the most likely direct consequence is:
A. stronger hydrophobic packing in the core.
B. destabilization of the protein core because the charged side chain is unfavorable in a nonpolar environment.
C. automatic formation of a new peptide bond.
D. conversion of the protein into a carbohydrate.

Correct answer: B — destabilization of the protein core because the charged side chain is unfavorable in a nonpolar environment. Explanation: Charged residues are generally favored at solvent-exposed surfaces rather than in tightly packed hydrophobic cores.
BIO-BCH-WAT-018 | Moderate | Application | Topic: Specific heat
Two equal masses of water and a nonpolar solvent receive the same amount of heat. The water temperature rises less. Which explanation is best?
A. Water molecules have no kinetic energy.
B. Covalent O-H bonds are continuously broken during ordinary heating.
C. The nonpolar solvent has more hydrogen bonds than water.
D. Some added energy is used to disrupt hydrogen bonds before increasing molecular motion.
E. Water has a lower molecular mass than all nonpolar solvents.
Correct answer: D — Some added energy is used to disrupt hydrogen bonds before increasing molecular motion. Explanation: Hydrogen bonding gives water a high heat capacity because heat is absorbed in disrupting intermolecular interactions.
BIO-BCH-WAT-019 | Moderate | Application | Topic: Ice density
A lake freezes at the surface while liquid water remains below. Which property of water contributes most directly to this pattern?
A. Liquid water becomes nonpolar below 0°C.
B. Solid water is less dense than liquid water and therefore remains at the surface.
C. Hydrogen bonding disappears completely in ice.
D. Ice has a greater density than liquid water.
E. Frozen water conducts all heat away instantly.
Correct answer: B — Solid water is less dense than liquid water and therefore remains at the surface. Explanation: The open lattice of ice makes it less dense, so the surface ice floats and insulates water beneath it.
BIO-BCH-WAT-020 | Moderate | Application | Topic: pH and proteins
An enzyme loses activity when pH changes from 7 to 3, even though its peptide bonds remain intact. The most likely explanation is that low pH:
A. converts every amino acid into glucose.
B. changes ionization of side chains and disrupts interactions needed for the active-site shape.
C. breaks all peptide bonds immediately.
D. removes all water from the enzyme.
E. creates new nucleotides inside the protein.
Correct answer: B — changes ionization of side chains and disrupts interactions needed for the active-site shape. Explanation: pH alters protonation states of side chains, which can disrupt ionic and hydrogen-bond interactions important for structure.
BIO-BCH-WAT-021 | Moderate | Conceptual | Topic: DNA stability
A DNA region rich in G-C base pairs generally requires more heat to separate its strands than an A-T-rich region because G-C pairs:
A. contain covalent bonds between the two DNA strands.
B. have larger phosphate groups.
C. form three hydrogen bonds rather than two.
D. are always located closer to the chromosome centromere.
E. have fewer atoms than A-T pairs.
Correct answer: C — form three hydrogen bonds rather than two. Explanation: G-C base pairs have three hydrogen bonds, so G-C-rich DNA generally has a higher melting temperature.
BIO-BCH-WAT-022 | Moderate | Application | Topic: Detergents
A detergent disrupts a cell membrane mainly because its amphipathic molecules:

D. make fatty-acid tails strongly ionic.
E. convert the bilayer into DNA.
Correct answer: B — interact with both water and membrane lipids, dispersing hydrophobic lipid assemblies. Explanation: Amphipathic detergents surround hydrophobic lipid regions while remaining soluble in water, disrupting bilayer organization.
BIO-BCH-WAT-023 | Moderate | Experimental | Topic: Hydrogen bonding
A mutation replaces a polar amino acid on a protein surface with a nonpolar amino acid. Which effect is most plausible?
A. The peptide backbone necessarily breaks at the mutation site.

C. The residue becomes positively charged in all conditions.

Correct answer: B — The new residue may form fewer favorable interactions with surrounding water. Explanation: Polar surface residues often hydrogen-bond with water, whereas nonpolar residues interact less favorably with the aqueous environment.
BIO-BCH-WAT-024 | Moderate | Conceptual | Topic: Weak interactions
Why can a protein be stable even though many interactions holding its tertiary structure are individually weak?
A. Weak interactions become covalent when enough are present.

D. Water cannot interact with proteins.
E. A large number of weak interactions can produce substantial collective stabilization.
Correct answer: E — A large number of weak interactions can produce substantial collective stabilization. Explanation: Protein stability often arises from the combined effect of many hydrogen bonds, ionic interactions and hydrophobic contacts.

BIO-BCH-WAT-025 | Hard | Upper-end IMAT | Integrated | Topic: Protein folding
A soluble protein has a hydrophobic core and polar surface. A mutation moves several nonpolar residues from the core to the surface without changing chain length. Which combination is most likely?
A. Greater solubility and stronger hydration of the mutated surface

BIO-BCH-WAT-026 | Hard | Upper-end IMAT | Integrated | Topic: pH and ionic interactions
A salt bridge in a protein forms between a negatively charged carboxylate side chain and a positively charged amino group. Strong acid is added. Which change most directly weakens this interaction?

B. The peptide bond becomes a glycosidic bond.
C. Hydrophobic residues become fully ionized.
D. Protonation of the carboxylate group reduces its negative charge.

Correct answer: D — Protonation of the carboxylate group reduces its negative charge. Explanation: Lower pH protonates carboxylates, reducing the electrostatic attraction that stabilizes the salt bridge.
BIO-BCH-WAT-027 | Hard | Upper-end IMAT | Integrated | Topic: Membrane hydrophobic effect

BIO-BCH-WAT-028 | Hard | Upper-end IMAT | Integrated | Topic: Hydrogen bonds in macromolecules

BIO-BCH-WAT-029 | Hard | Upper-end IMAT | Application | Topic: Water and thermoregulation
An animal exposed to dry heat can maintain body temperature more effectively by sweating than an identical animal in saturated humid air. The main reason is that:

BIO-BCH-WAT-030 | Hard | Upper-end IMAT | Experimental | Topic: Water solvent properties

BIO-BCH-WAT-031 | Hard | Ahsora Challenge | Integrated | Topic: Hydrophobic effect
A membrane protein is removed from its lipid bilayer and placed directly into water without detergent. Its transmembrane region contains many leucine and valine residues. What is the most likely outcome?

BIO-BCH-WAT-032 | Hard | Ahsora Challenge | Integrated | Topic: Weak interactions and stability
